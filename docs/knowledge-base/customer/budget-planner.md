# Budget Planner: where to spend for better results

## How to compare advertising spend and get a budget recommendation

Open Analysis > Budget Planner to compare where to spend money toward a
measurable business result. For example, choose Shopify store revenue or orders
as the result, and Meta Ads spend and Google Ads spend as separate spending
options. This is not limited to those providers: use any eligible datasets or
separate channel values in a dimension column. Choose a total budget, currency,
start Monday and number of planning weeks, then Preview budget.

The planner checks whether the data can support an allocation before showing
a suggested mix. If the evidence passes, compare the recent mix and suggested
test at the same total budget, with an estimated additional result and model
range. Save the plan after preview, then Create Decision to record the action,
expected outcome and review date. It never changes an advertising platform's
budget automatically. These estimates are not proof that a channel caused
sales and do not guarantee that moving money will improve results.

## Select the result and spending options

Select one additive result to improve, such as revenue or orders, and two to six
monetary spending options. Each selection has a dataset, metric, date column,
and optional dimension column and unique value. One dataset can contain several
options, such as spend for Channel A and Channel B. These options must use
non-overlapping values of the same dimension. Do not include a total alongside
categories already included in that total, or duplicate spend across sources.

All spending must use the chosen currency; Decisionate does not convert
currencies here. A recognized currency mismatch blocks the plan. When currency
metadata is absent, your confirmation is required. Use a single store-level
outcome, not the sum of multiple platforms' independently attributed revenue.
Those platforms can claim the same sale.

The budget is for the entire plan, not each week. Plans cover one to thirteen
weeks and start on a Monday within four weeks. Optional minimum and maximum
amounts apply to each option over that whole period. The first version keeps
each option within 20% of its recent eight-week average spending, scaled to the
planning period. A substantially different budget may therefore be unsupported.

## Evidence needed before recommending a spending mix

The planner uses up to the latest three years of history and requires at least
104 complete matching weeks. The latest complete shared week must be no more
than two weeks old. Daily inputs need every day's record, including explicit
zero-activity days. Alternatively, choose complete weekly totals labeled with
their Monday start dates. Missing records are not filled with zero. Month-only
dates, monthly source history, historical demo files and unresolved gaps cannot
support a current weekly allocation.

Each option needs varying spending that is sufficiently independent from the
other options. The model includes annual seasonal patterns and a trend, tries
several carryover assumptions, and checks predictions against 32 later weeks
held out in two forward-moving tests. It must perform better than the baseline
without spending, pass numerical checks and show a positive improvement across
the tested uncertainty and timing checks. Passing these checks does not prove
causation or eliminate omitted influences.

Up to three additional additive business influences can be supplied. Only use
variables not caused by the spending itself. Consider promotions, availability,
competition and major business changes before confirming the business context.
The model cannot certify that you have captured every important influence.

## Interpret the allocation and uncertainty

The gray bars show the recent spending proportions at your proposed total
budget. Green bars show the suggested test. Both totals match, so estimated
additional results reflect a change in mix rather than simply more money.
Spending is modeled evenly across the planning weeks. Any gap before the plan
assumes the recent spending level continues. Supplied business influences stay
at their recent average.

The displayed model range is based on resampled historical residuals. It does
not cover every uncertainty about the model, future events or missing business
influences, and it is not a guaranteed sales forecast. Prefer a limited,
measurable test with a review date. If evidence is weak, the planner explains
what is missing and withholds allocations rather than inventing percentages.

## Save plans and create decisions

Only a completed, supported preview can be saved. Repeated saves of the same
preview return the same plan; a new preview can be saved as a separate plan.
Changing selections invalidates the unsaved preview. Saved plans are historical
snapshots, not live recommendations; preview again after data or dates change.
Delete a saved plan from its delete button. Its linked decision is preserved.

Create Decision becomes available after saving. Record the specific action,
expected result and a review date after the planning period. Repeated creation
for the same plan opens the existing decision. The plan's selections, filters,
allocation, model limits and evidence are retained with the decision. A saved
plan does not itself mean the action was taken.

Budget Planner handles budget allocation scenarios. It is not inventory,
product assortment or staffing optimization, and it does not calculate profit
unless an eligible profit result is actually supplied. Help & Support explains
the workflow but does not inspect your private datasets or determine your
actual allocation in chat.

## Can I plan spending by campaign, region or product instead of ad platform?

Budget Planner is generic: the spending choices can be campaigns, regions or
other non-overlapping groups, not only named advertising platforms. Select one
additive result and two to six monetary spending series. Separate values in one
dimension column can supply several options from a single dataset. Keep dates,
currency and metric definitions consistent, and do not count an overall total
again alongside its components.

Each option still needs enough independent spending variation and complete,
recent history. A product's sales alone is not a spending option. This is not a
general product-mix, price, inventory-ordering or staffing optimizer. For past
product performance, use Profitability; for past channel returns, use Return on
spend. A future allocation is a scenario to test, not a historical attribution.

## Why will Budget Planner not recommend a mix for my new business?

The planner needs at least 104 complete matching weeks within its three-year
history, with the latest shared week no more than two weeks old. It also needs
spending options that varied independently enough to distinguish them. A new
business, old demo file, monthly-only history, missing dates, or channels always
increased together may not support a recommendation. Passing the history count
alone is not enough: the model's prediction and uncertainty checks must pass too.

It explains the missing evidence rather than inventing a percentage split. Keep
sources current and records complete. Return on spend and Profitability can
review suitable recorded amounts over a shorter period, but cannot manufacture
an optimal future allocation. Do not duplicate records, treat missing days as
zero or interpret a correlation coefficient as a budget percentage.

## Can I double the budget or automatically apply the suggested allocation?

Budget Planner does not automatically change a platform's spending. A supported
preview can be saved and used to create a decision, with a specific action and
review date. You carry out any approved business change separately.

The total budget applies to the entire one-to-thirteen-week plan, not each week.
Minimum and maximum amounts also apply over the full plan. The supported range
keeps each option within 20% of its recent eight-week average, scaled to that
duration. A much larger increase, such as doubling spending, can therefore be
outside the supported range. The recent mix and suggested test use the same
total budget; any estimated difference is not simply the effect of more money.
Start with a bounded test and review actual results; the estimate is not a
guarantee or proof that the suggested mix caused sales.
